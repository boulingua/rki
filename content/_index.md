## What this is

An openly licensed course in Russian as a foreign language, written for the
German *Gesamtschule* system and for independent adult learners. It assumes no
prior contact with the language and no prior contact with the alphabet. The
whole course is published as a static site: every unit is a readable HTML
article, and every unit ships the same set of classroom artefacts — a slide
deck, a printable worksheet, an answer key, and audio recorded segment by
segment.

The course is authored, not aggregated. Texts, dialogues, exercises and exam
stimuli are written for this course; quoted material is restricted to sources
in the public domain or under an open licence, and is cited.

## Who this is for

Two audiences, one body of material. Teachers of Russian at a comprehensive
school who need a curriculum-aligned skeleton they can teach from on Monday,
and self-studiers who want a structured, exam-backed path rather than a
vocabulary app. The unit is the unit of work: a single unit fills a double
lesson, a level fills a school year or an intensive semester.

## How a unit is built

Every unit follows the same five moves, so that the shape of the lesson is
predictable and the teacher can improvise on the content rather than on the
structure.

1. **Activate** — recover what is already known; a picture, a question, a
   two-minute exchange.
2. **Input** — the model text, dialogue or paradigm, with the vocabulary that
   carries it.
3. **Practise** — controlled tasks with answer keys, differentiated for
   support and extension.
4. **Apply** — the productive task: writing, speaking, role-play, mediation.
5. **Reflect** — self-assessment against the can-do statements the unit claims.

Exams are not an afterthought glued to the last unit. Each level closes with a
model exam published as a first-class page — reading, listening, use of
language, writing — with a duration, a total, and a marking scale, and a
printable PDF alongside it.

## Conventions of this course

- **Cyrillic from the first minute.** Transliteration appears only as a
  temporary crutch inside Level 0 and the earliest A1 units, and is gone by
  the end of A1.
- **Stress is marked.** Every multisyllabic word in teaching text carries the
  acute — `хорошо́`, `спаси́бо`, `говори́ть` — because Russian stress is lexical,
  mobile, unmarked in ordinary writing, and decides how the vowels are
  pronounced. Marks are dropped only in the authentic reading passages from B1
  onwards, where learning to read without them is the point.
- **ё is always written ё.** Never е. Russian print routinely conflates the
  two; a course that does the same teaches a wrong pronunciation and a wrong
  stress.
- **Standard, contemporary, neutral.** Moscow-standard literary Russian in a
  neutral register. No regional or heritage variants, no textbook-only idiom
  nobody says.

## Materials and audio

Slide decks and worksheets are generated from the shared branded LaTeX
templates and committed to the repository as PDFs, so that the published site
never depends on a TeX installation. Audio is generated with the shared
Piper-based pipeline using two Russian voices — one female, one male — so that
dialogues alternate speakers, and is published as Opus with a visible
transcript under every player. The [materials hub]({{< relref "/materials" >}})
collects them by kind; each unit page also links its own.

## State of the course

The site is at its first milestone: the structure, the design system, the
legal pages and the build pipeline are in place, and the five level sections
are described but not yet populated. Level 0 is the first body of teaching
material to be published, followed by A1; the course is flagged as active on
the boulingua language map when Level 0 and A1 are complete, and A2 to B2
follow as ongoing releases. The plan, including what is deliberately not
claimed, is in [`ROADMAP.md`](https://github.com/boulingua/rki/blob/main/ROADMAP.md).
