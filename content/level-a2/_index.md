## The two hard things

**Aspect.** Every Russian verb belongs to a pair, and choosing between the
members of that pair is not a matter of tense but of how the speaker views the
event: as a process or as a whole with a result. Neither German nor English
has a clean analogue, which is why the distinction is introduced here on its
own terms — in the past tense first, where the contrast is sharpest — and then
returned to at every level afterwards. A2 teaches the pairs, the core
oppositions and the reliable prefixal patterns; it does not pretend the matter
is closed.

**The remaining cases.** Genitive, dative and instrumental complete the six.
Each gets a unit rather than a table: genitive with negation, quantity and
possession; dative with the recipient, age and the whole family of impersonal
constructions; instrumental with means, accompaniment and the predicate after
быть. By the end of the level a learner has met all six cases in all three
genders and both numbers, receptively and productively.

Each unit ships an HTML article, a slide deck, a printable worksheet, an
answer key and native-voice audio, with two alternating voices in the
dialogues.

## The A2 exam

A model exam in the format of the level: reading, listening, use of language
and writing, with a duration, a point total and a marking scale, published as
a page and as a printable PDF. Stimuli are written for this course.

## Curriculum alignment

A2 is the second of the three levels covered by the course's declared `core`
conformance. Every in-scope CEFR scale carrying an official A2 descriptor is
implemented in a unit; scales with no A2 descriptor are recorded as
`no-official-descriptor` in the course's conformance manifest.

{{< callout type="note" title="Conventions" >}}
No transliteration from this level onwards. Stress marked with the acute on
every multisyllabic word in teaching text; **ё** always written as **ё**.
{{< /callout >}}
