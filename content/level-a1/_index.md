## The through-line

The whole course follows one thread. **Аня** is a student who has just arrived
in a Russian-speaking city; her flatmates, her university, her first job and
her first journeys supply the situations. Because the cast and the setting
recur, vocabulary recurs with them: the kitchen introduced in the unit on the
home comes back in the unit on food, and again in A2 when the past tense
arrives. Each unit names the figures it uses.

## What A1 does with the grammar

Russian nominal morphology — six cases across three genders and two numbers,
with animacy cutting across them — is the dominant load of the whole course.
It is spiralled, not front-loaded. A1 takes three cases and stops:

- **Nominative** as the dictionary form and the subject.
- **Accusative** for the direct object, including the animacy split that makes
  masculine animates look genitive.
- **Prepositional** for location and for talking *about* something — the case
  that never appears without its preposition.

Genitive, dative and instrumental are A2. Verbal **aspect** is present in A1
only lexically: pairs are learnt as vocabulary, in fixed phrases, and the
imperfective/perfective distinction is not yet analysed. Teaching aspect
before the past tense exists is a well-known way to lose a class.

Each unit ships an HTML article, a slide deck, a printable worksheet, an
answer key and native-voice audio for its vocabulary and dialogues.

## The A1 exam

The level closes with a **model exam** as a first-class page and a printable
PDF: reading, listening, use of language and writing, with a stated duration,
a point total and a marking scale (*Notenschlüssel*). The exam stimuli are
written for this course. It is a model in the format of the level, not a
reproduction of any examination board's material, and passing it certifies
nothing.

## Curriculum alignment

Every unit declares CEFR can-do statements in its front matter, and each
statement resolves to a descriptor identifier in the shared boulingua
curriculum framework, which is grounded in the CEFR Companion Volume. The
target for A1 is complete coverage: every in-scope scale that carries an
official A1 descriptor is implemented, and every scale that carries none is
recorded as `no-official-descriptor`. A missing scale is a fault; an absent
descriptor is not.

{{< callout type="note" title="Conventions" >}}
Cyrillic throughout; transliteration survives from Level 0 into the first
units and disappears by the end of this level. Stress marked with the acute on
every multisyllabic word; **ё** always written as **ё**.
